Linear stability lobes can overestimate the usable depth of cut when an active damping actuator saturates. This computational study asks how much disturbance a linearly stable, actively damped milling cut tolerates before saturation triggers chatter. In a single-mode model of a published case study (17 of 19 tests classified as measured), the command splits into a forced part and a residual authority, which governs the basin near the limit. An abrupt entry chatters below 97\,\% of the linear limit at 157 of 251 speeds, and near the limit small kicks cause bounded clipped chatter. In 56 further published tests on the same rig, losses of stability between the uncontrolled and controlled limits occurred only with saturation; the model misses them. A circle criterion, its frequency condition proved by interval arithmetic, shows the forced orbit of the averaged delay model to attract every solution below 0.97 of the uncontrolled limit (median); above it, linear matrix inequalities give inner estimates of the basin, numerically verified for collocation models. A basin-aware gain schedule, grid-searched at 21 speeds, gives the largest depth that is linearly stable, keeps a voltage margin and is certified against a required disturbance. With equal gain range and margins it certifies 6\,mm more depth than authority-based gains for 5\,mm/s, hardly more for 1\,mm/s; with abrupt entry or a 16\,mm cap it cuts at most 0.6\,mm deeper. Under model errors its points, nearer their linear limit, fail more often (10--17\,\% against 7\,\%).
